# Cutting Stock Problem

The cutting stock problem arises in many manufacturing and logistics settings where large raw materials must be cut into smaller pieces to meet specific demands while minimizing waste.

A natural example comes from the paper and printing industry. A printing company purchases large rolls of paper, each with a fixed width. Customers place orders for smaller rolls of various widths and quantities. The company must decide how to cut each large roll into smaller ones so that all customer orders are satisfied. Because any unused paper becomes waste (or scrap), the goal is to plan the cutting patterns in a way that minimizes the total amount of leftover paper, or equivalently, minimizes the number of large rolls used.
