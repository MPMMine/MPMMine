# Carton Design Optimization

This problem concerns a printing firm that produces diverse packaging for food items and magazines. The firm creates
cartons with identical dimensions, but varies the printed content and colors within those cartons. For instance,
different food product variations might feature distinct text and background colors. The firm seeks to determine the
most efficient way to produce these carton designs, minimizing waste. Specifically, they need to decide how many
distinct template sheets to create and which variations to include on each template, aiming for the lowest possible
waste levels across one, two, or more templates.

To streamline the solving process, symmetry-breaking constraints are applied to the variations; variations with identical demand are ordered lexicographically by their template slot allocations, while variations with lower demand are constrained to have total printed amounts less than or equal to those with higher demand. Additionally, implied constraints are utilized to limit overproduction and surplus. The surplus of any single variation, as well as the cumulative surplus of the first several variations, is strictly restricted from exceeding the overall total surplus. Finally, the model defines structural implied constraints on the template run lengths based on the total number of templates used, imposing specific upper and lower bounds on the number of pressings for each individual template when two or three templates are active.
