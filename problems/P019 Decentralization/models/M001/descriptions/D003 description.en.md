# Decentralization

A major organization is considering relocating a subset of its operational units away from its primary headquarters location. Each unit would generate a quantified advantage (such as lower living expenses, public-sector subsidies, or improved hiring prospects) depending on which specific city it occupies. These advantages have been estimated and recorded for every unit–city pairing. On the other hand, relocating units creates additional inter-unit communication expenses. The volume of communication traffic between every pair of units, as well as the per-unit communication charge between every pair of cities, have likewise been quantified in advance. The task is to assign each unit to one of the available cities (the headquarters city being one of them) in order to minimize the total annual expenditure, where total expenditure is the aggregate of all pairwise communication charges (each pair counted once) minus the sum of all relocation advantages collected. Additionally, no single city—whether the headquarters or any satellite location—may be assigned more than a prescribed upper bound on the number of units it can accommodate.
